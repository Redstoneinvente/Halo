# Touchpad Notch

In the direct-download edition, open **Settings → Touchpad Notch** and enable monitoring. It is off by default. Press **Record spot**, lift all fingers, then hold one finger still on the desired physical touchpad spot for one second. Recording times out after 15 seconds and can be cancelled. If the touchpad is already idle, the next touch can be recorded immediately; only fingers already down when Record is pressed must lift first. Record is unavailable when the input source could not start. A diagram shows the saved zone, and **Spot size** adjusts its tolerance. Coordinates and the device identifier persist locally in UserDefaults; raw touch history is not saved or sent to CIs.

Choose **Halo notch** to toggle the existing surface on the pointer's display (falling back to Halo's main display). Choose **Touchpad CI** for the included compact media interface. Install that interface explicitly in these settings and grant media information/control separately. It uses the ordinary SDK 0.1 validator, renderer, permissions and priority arbitration. It requires Halo Full, the package enabled, and Custom CI globally enabled; a higher-priority owner may decline activation. The physical touchpad input is a user-requested manual activation, not a new package trigger type. No SDK schema changes are introduced.

**Hold to open** and **Hold to close** are independently adjustable from 0.1 to 5 seconds. A continuous touch can activate only once: lift before toggling again. Moving outside the zone or dropping below the pressure threshold restarts the hold; multiple fingers block activation until all fingers lift. Missing/stale input does not complete a hold. Monitoring stops on disable, runtime lock, session inactivity, sleep and quit. Wake resumes enabled monitoring with fresh recognition state. Use **Retry touchpad connection** after connecting a touchpad; re-record if its device identifier changes.

**Required pressure** uses the force field from the saved device's contact frames, independently of pointer location or frontmost app. It never substitutes contact size, total capacitance or density. Values are normalized to a relative scale (255 raw units until calibrated); they are not force in physical units or AppKit pressure stages. **Record pressure** waits for a fresh single-finger press on the saved spot. Hold your chosen firmness steady for three seconds while the progress bar/countdown runs; two haptic taps confirm the pressure was saved. A change greater than 20% (with a three-unit minimum tolerance), lifting, leaving the spot or lost input restarts the hold. Calibration confirmation is always haptic on compatible hardware, independently of the normal activation-feedback toggle. The lower 20th percentile of the stable samples reduces sensitivity to small variations. The learned level becomes 80% on the relative slider. A 40 ms time-based filter smooths readings, a release band of up to three percentage points avoids threshold jitter, and zero force, lift, missing force, another finger or lost input cannot retain pressure from a previous contact. The live meter refreshes at up to 30 Hz. Activation is suspended during calibration; release is required after capture. A 15-second timeout/cancel keeps the previous preference intact. Existing settings without a learned reference remain compatible. **Any** permits touch-only activation. On trackpads without force readings a nonzero threshold prevents activation. Haptics require compatible hardware.

System-wide physical coordinates currently require Apple's private MultitouchSupport framework. The bridge is compiled only for `HALO_DIRECT`, opens a fixed system framework path, bounds/copies callback data, checks the contact ABI size and required symbols, identifies touchpads from the IOKit Product property (OpaqueSurface is not a reliable trackpad flag), and unregisters callbacks on shutdown. This is independent of the declarative CI package system. The App Store build displays an availability explanation and contains no MultitouchSupport bridge. No downloaded/package native code is loaded. Touchpad Notch does not suppress clicks or pointer movement; normal interactions may still reach the frontmost application.

## Validation

`TouchpadNotchTests` covers hold duration, separate close duration, release rearming, zone exit, finger replacement, multi-touch rejection, absent/insufficient pressure, input gaps, external surface changes, nonfinite coordinates, persistence/normalization and the bundled CI's existing SDK contract. Build both Halo Direct and Halo App Store. A physical-device acceptance pass should additionally check calibration, stationary holds, pressure reporting in frontmost and background apps, actual haptics, device reconnection and sleep/wake. An automated build cannot verify tactile feedback or global pressure delivery.


Recording regressions are covered by `TouchpadRecordingTests`: Apple internal/Magic Trackpad identification, starting while idle without an empty callback, releasing the finger used to press Record, an idle second device, interrupted input and motion/multiple-finger resets. The direct input source was also started and stopped against a real built-in Apple trackpad after removing the erroneous OpaqueSurface filter.


`TouchpadPressureTests` covers relative normalization, calibration migration, jitter filtering, immediate zero-force release, invalid/missing force, per-contact isolation, continuous calibration, three-second steady holds, restart on changed firmness and threshold hysteresis. The private contact force ABI at offset 52 is also exposed as `pressure` in [OpenMultitouchSupport](https://github.com/Kyome22/OpenMultitouchSupport/blob/main/Framework/OpenMultitouchSupportXCF/OpenMTInternal.h). No raw readings are saved except the explicit learned reference.
